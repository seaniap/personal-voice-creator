# Interview Guide

Ask these one at a time, conversationally. React briefly to each answer before moving on. Use the language selected in Phase 0 — do not switch languages mid-interview. The goal is to surface how they actually write, not to fill a form.

---

## Q1 — The anti-goal (warm-up)

> When you write a letter or an article, what is the one feeling you most DON'T want the reader to have?

Examples to offer if they're stuck: feeling you're making small talk, that you're cold, that you're showing off, that you're reading from a script, that you're being preachy or clichéd.

*Why this question:* the anti-goal defines the voice faster than the goal. Knowing what someone refuses to sound like sharply narrows the style.

---

## Q2 — Structure-first or feel-first

> When you write, which are you more like:
> A) Plan the structure first, then write — logical, orderly
> B) Follow the feeling, write as it comes, tidy up afterwards
> C) Both, depending on mood or task

*Follow-up:* if they pick C, ask which task pulls them toward A and which toward B.

---

## Q3 — Audience switching

> Do you change your tone a lot for different audiences? For example — writing to a client vs. a friend vs. someone senior vs. someone in another language. Do you become a completely different person, or is the core the same and only the formality shifts?

*Capture:* the specific audiences they name. These become the "modes" in voice-style.md. Ask about language switching here too (do they mix English / Japanese / etc.?).

---

## Q4 — Rhythm and formatting habits

> Are there sentence-rhythm or formatting habits you already know about yourself? For instance:
> - short sentences to close, for a beat of pause
> - conclusion first, then explanation
> - questions to lead into the next section
> - small concrete examples rather than big abstractions
> - mixing in English or another language
> - preferring visual / diagram-style explanation for complex things

*Capture:* whatever they recognize. Don't force it — partial answers are fine, samples will fill gaps.

---

## Q5 — Real samples (the most valuable input)

> Is there anything you've written — an article, a letter, a post — that you look at and think "yes, this is the most me"? If so, just paste it, drop the link, or upload the file. If not, no problem, we can skip and add samples later.

*Action:* fetch URLs, read uploads, or use pasted text. Extract: opening/closing conventions, sentence length, example style, language mix, punctuation. These observations carry more weight than anything from Q1–Q4.

---

## Optional deep-dive questions (use if they want a richer profile)

- Who do you write to most often? (anchors the default audience)
- Is there a word, phrase, or opening you catch yourself overusing — or one you refuse to use?
- When you re-read your own draft, what's the first thing you cut?
- For business writing specifically: what's your default greeting and sign-off?
- For another language: what's your real proficiency level, so we don't reach for vocabulary you wouldn't use?
